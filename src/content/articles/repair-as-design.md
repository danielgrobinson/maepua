Every object enters a second phase after its first failure. Good design determines whether that moment begins a repair, a compromise, or a trip to the bin.

## Visible decisions

Repair-friendly construction does not require every object to look industrial. It requires decisions about access, fastening, documentation, and replacement to be made before those decisions become expensive.

Start with the likely failure points. Can a person reach them? Can parts be separated without destroying adjacent material? Are uncommon tools actually necessary?

## Maintenance is a relationship

An object that can be maintained invites its owner to understand it. That understanding can create attachment more reliably than surface novelty.
