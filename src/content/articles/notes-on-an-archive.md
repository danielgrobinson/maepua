An archive should reduce future confusion. If maintaining it creates more work than the project itself, the system will eventually be abandoned.

## Keep the original context

Record the date, the purpose, a few constraints, and the names of the files that matter. A short explanation written at the time is often more valuable than a polished reconstruction written months later.

Photograph prototypes before taking them apart. Keep failed versions when they explain why the final approach changed.

## Use boring formats

Plain text, common image formats, and predictable folders age well. The archive can still have a beautiful public surface while its underlying material remains simple and portable.
